TITLE: The full Gaia colour-magnitude diagram with white dwarfs reveals a declining Milky Way star formation history
ABSTRACT: White dwarfs (WDs) provide a fossil record of the Milky Way star formation history (SFH) through the combined effects of stellar evolution and cooling. They have, however, generally been modelled in isolation from the non-degenerate population, so that no single self-consistent set of Galactic assumptions has been required to reproduce both. We present CALOMERA, a semi-analytic chemo-dynamical population synthesis framework in which the vertical density profiles of mono-age subpopulations are computed self-consistently with the Galactic potential and coupled to modern WD cooling sequences, metallicity-dependent progenitor lifetimes and synthetic photometry. We calibrate 18 parameters describing the initial mass function, SFH, initial-final mass relation and WD population against a Gaia DR3 sample of $7.98\times10^{6}$ stars within 1 kpc, including $1.1\times10^{5}$ WDs. The calibrated model reproduces the star counts in each colour-magnitude diagram region to within 3% and the total number count to within 0.5%, compared with the 81% excess in the predicted WD count obtained with the previous main-sequence-only calibration. The recovered SFH peaks early, declines exponentially thereafter, and includes two recent bursts, located at $τ\approx2.4$ and $0.5$ Gyr. Three cooling libraries (Montréal, BaSTI and LPCODE) yield comparably good fits, with differences confined to ${\rm M_G}\gtrsim15$ mag. The predicted luminosity function is in reasonable agreement with the 100 pc Gaia WD sample. We further predict the Galactic WD distribution and differential number counts for surveys of varying depth. Extending the framework to the substellar regime using ATMO models, we predict the Galactic L, T, and Y dwarf populations and their contribution to the deep JWST fields.

TITLE: Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding
ABSTRACT: How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.

TITLE: Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution
ABSTRACT: Context attribution asks how removing parts of a prompt changes a language model's output. We study leave-one-out (LOO) attribution for a prompt of $n$ tokens and a fixed measurement function. Computing all LOO values directly requires $n$ model evaluations. Under a stability assumption, our algorithm estimates all LOO values to error proportional to $\varepsilon$ with constant probability using $O(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ evaluation calls. To complement our upper bound, we prove a matching $Ω(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ lower bound on the number of evaluation calls in an oracle model. Empirically, we verify coefficient decay consistent with stability and evaluate the performance of our sketching algorithm on a small language-model retrieval task. Technically, our result is based on a sketching algorithm for arithmetic circuits whose Taylor coefficients satisfy the stability condition recently introduced by Gunn [Gun26]. A direct adaptation of their forward differentiation sketches would require $\widetilde{O}(\log(n)/\varepsilon^2)$ evaluation calls. Our key observation is that we can apply forward differentiation to the backpropagation circuit, with only constant overhead over a forward sketch of the same Taylor degree. This reduces the variance of the sketches and is key to our improved evaluation bound. Our lower bound builds on the techniques of Amsel, Chen, Duman Keles, Halikias, Musco, and Musco [ACDH+26, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications] for lower bounding the number of matrix-vector queries to estimate the diagonal elements of random matrices.

TITLE: The Tournament Equilibrium Set Is PSPACE-Complete, Even under Hereditary Uniqueness
ABSTRACT: The tournament equilibrium set (TEQ), proposed by Schwartz in 1990, is a classical voting rule based on pairwise majority comparisons. Its definition is recursive: an alternative is judged by the alternatives that beat it, called its dominators, and by which of those dominators TEQ itself selects. Deciding whether a given alternative belongs to TEQ was known to be NP-hard and to lie in PSPACE. We close this gap by proving that the problem is PSPACE-complete. To our knowledge, this is the first PSPACE-completeness result for membership in a tournament solution. The hardness persists even when every nonempty subtournament has a unique minimal retentive set, the property conjectured by Schwartz to hold for all tournaments. Moreover, every such subtournament in our hard instances contains an alternative with at most three dominators. We prove that a single alternative with at most four dominators suffices both to guarantee uniqueness and to find a TEQ winner in $O(N^2)$ time. Thus, on our hard instances, finding one winner is efficient even though recognizing a specified winner is PSPACE-complete. We also study how the complexity changes with the dominator bound. If every nonempty subtournament has an alternative with at most one dominator, the entire TEQ can be computed in quadratic time; allowing two dominators makes membership NP-hard. Under the same condition with four dominators, all $t$ winners can be computed in $O(2^t N^2)$ time, where $N$ is the number of alternatives.

ABSTRACT: Many algorithmic frameworks exist for evaluating join queries. TITLE: Towards a Theory of Learnability via Debate
ABSTRACT: Debate has been recently proposed as a training protocol that is hoped to produce more aligned models even as model capabilities scale. The idea is that the human, instead of providing supervision in the form of labels, judges a debate between two models that compete to prove the correctness of opposite labels. It can be shown that functions of a much higher computational complexity can be correctly judged in this way, compared to the complexity of functions that can be directly evaluated using a computationally weak evaluator. This suggests that perhaps debate can be used to teach very complicated functions to models. However, previous work has largely ignored the fact that this is a training protocol, and simply analyzes the game being played between the two debaters for a known judge. We take the first steps towards understanding the learning problem implicit within the protocol, by formulating it as an online learning problem. We show that once we formulate the learning problem precisely, there are cases where the best way to learn given access to a judge is different from running an adversarial debate. This motivates us to define a cooperative version of the protocol, whose learnability we characterize using a combinatorial dimension. We also consider the possibility of the human judge being fallible and occasionally judging the debate incorrectly. We show that a small number of errors in judgement can lead to a large error in learning.

TITLE: The Submodular Secretary Problem: Tight $1-1/e$ for Monotone Objectives, and Strictly Between $1/e$ and $1/2$ in General
ABSTRACT: In the submodular secretary problem with a cardinality constraint, $n$ elements arrive in uniformly random order, and an algorithm must irrevocably accept or reject each one on arrival so as to maximize a nonnegative submodular function over sets of at most $k$ elements. For monotone objectives we settle the problem: the optimal competitive ratio tends to exactly $1-1/e$ as $k\to\infty$, and its worst case over all budgets $k$ is exactly $1/e$. The bound $1-1/e$ cannot be exceeded even with unlimited computation, and it is achieved by a single algorithm with at most $4n+1$ value queries; previously the best ratio without a shortlist was $1/e-o(1)$, with exponentially many queries. For general objectives, where the best known ratio was about $0.107$, we show that the optimal limiting ratio lies strictly between $1/e$ and $1/2$: we achieve $1/e+3.5\cdot10^{-6}$ for large $k$, and no algorithm achieves more than $0.497053$. We also achieve $0.2677$ at every budget. The main new ingredient of our algorithm is eligibility attenuation: an element that passes the price test at time $t$ is accepted with probability $e^{-τ}$, where $τ$ is the total earlier time during which it would have passed, which makes the probability that it has already been selected exactly $1-e^{-τ}$.

TITLE: The Tournament Equilibrium Set Is PSPACE-Complete, Even under Hereditary Uniqueness
ABSTRACT: The tournament equilibrium set (TEQ), proposed by Schwartz in 1990, is a classical voting rule based on pairwise majority comparisons. Its definition is recursive: an alternative is judged by the alternatives that beat it, called its dominators, and by which of those dominators TEQ itself selects. Deciding whether a given alternative belongs to TEQ was known to be NP-hard and to lie in PSPACE. We close this gap by proving that the problem is PSPACE-complete. To our knowledge, this is the first PSPACE-completeness result for membership in a tournament solution. The hardness persists even when every nonempty subtournament has a unique minimal retentive set, the property conjectured by Schwartz to hold for all tournaments. Moreover, every such subtournament in our hard instances contains an alternative with at most three dominators. We prove that a single alternative with at most four dominators suffices both to guarantee uniqueness and to find a TEQ winner in $O(N^2)$ time. Thus, on our hard instances, finding one winner is efficient even though recognizing a specified winner is PSPACE-complete. We also study how the complexity changes with the dominator bound. If every nonempty subtournament has an alternative with at most one dominator, the entire TEQ can be computed in quadratic time; allowing two dominators makes membership NP-hard. Under the same condition with four dominators, all $t$ winners can be computed in $O(2^t N^2)$ time, where $N$ is the number of alternatives.

ABSTRACT: We investigate whether and how LLMs encode goals: internal neural representations that precede and guide action, and that are flexible enough to be realized through many different execution paths. We construct a coding setting with multiple sub-tasks that allows us to evaluate goal-seeking behavior and the selection of sub-goals. In this setting, we localize an encoding of the next sub-goal in the outputs of a sparse set of attention heads. The same heads can be used to decode the LM's future actions in natural language (e.g., "I'm checking if a word is a palindrome"). We verify that this encoding is causal for next action and is plastic: transplanting it into a different environment, e.g., another programming language, triggers the same operation realized with a different sequence of tokens. The LM also sustains the encoding throughout the action's execution, which is puzzling because a transformer LM without recurrence cannot carry a state forward for more tokens than it has layers left. We propose that it does not need to. Under a non-recurrent goal-seeking (NRGS) account, the LM frequently re-reads the goal from the task specification, compares it with the progress visible in the growing context, and pursues what remains. We test the predictions of this account with targeted causal interventions.

TITLE: Radiation, Thermochemistry, and Stellar Feedback in DISPATCH
ABSTRACT: The growth of massive stars depends on whether their accretion flows survive radiation and outflow feedback from the forming stellar population. We present an extension of DISPATCH that couples directional stellar radiation, diffuse infrared transport, and thermochemistry to self-gravitating magnetohydrodynamics, individual stellar accretion, and protostellar jets. This AMR-based method uses DISPATCH's local timestepping, allowing stellar environments and their larger scale cluster environments to step on separate timescales, and providing the framework needed to scale to long-duration simulations of GMCs. Direct radiation is transported in multiple bands, including both ionizing and nonionizing radiation, and uses short characteristics to maintain source directions and shadowing. Diffuse radiation is implemented for IR reprocessed light, with M1 and stationary ray options for transport. The thermochemical model includes atomic and molecular hydrogen and helium chemistry, and metal cooling and dust. It also includes a composition-dependent equation of state. Stellar masses grow through accretion of gas rather than a prescribed IMF, allowing stellar masses to respond to the evolving gas supply and feedback. In tests, multiple-source illumination retains geometric shadows while diffuse reprocessing heats shielded material, and tests of jets, ionization fronts, and radiation pressure demonstrate expected behavior and convergence. Ionization fronts also cross asynchronous refinement boundaries with late-time propagation speeds within a few percent of analytic predictions. Together, these developments provide a framework for determining how radiative and mechanical feedback modify the duration and efficiency of accretion from turbulent clouds, connecting the gas supply to individual stars with the high-mass stellar initial mass function.

TITLE: SiC-BN composites with extreme thermal and environmental stability
ABSTRACT: Ceramic materials that maintain structural integrity and resist thermal-shock under extreme conditions are critical for many applications. Here we show that spark plasma sintered composites containing equal weight fractions of hexagonal silicon carbide (6H-SiC) and boron nitride (h-BN) exhibit exceptional stability under extreme high-temperature oxidizing conditions and unusually stable temperature-independent thermal conductivity across a broad temperature range. Under high-velocity oxygen-fuel torch exposure in air, it withstands prolonged heating and thermal-cycling at surface temperatures reaching &gt;1500 °C, whereas pure 6H-SiC and h-BN rapidly fractures or erodes. This lightweight, easily machinable ceramic composite will be a promising structural material in extreme environment applications.

TITLE: Active-space construction in single-fragment coupled-cluster embedding for chemical reactions
ABSTRACT: We benchmark single-fragment quantum embedding, in which electron correlation---at the level of coupled-cluster theory with single, double, and perturbative triple excitations [CCSD(T)]---is restricted to orbitals associated with a chosen molecular fragment, and the remaining orbitals are frozen at the Hartree-Fock level. We compare several methods for constructing fragment active spaces, and we test their performance on 60 organic reaction energies and three reaction profiles. A common fragment projection operator is used to relate several of the tested orbital constructions. Regional embedding, based on density matrix embedding theory, is shown to give accurate and robust performance when used for both the occupied and virtual active orbitals. For virtual orbitals, fragment-centered projected atomic orbitals and fragment natural orbitals (defined by eigenvectors of a fragment MP2 density matrix) also perform well, but the latter typically retains larger virtual spaces. Composite MP2 corrections yield the largest improvement: combined with fragment natural orbitals, they give average reaction energy errors below 0.3 kcal/mol relative to full-system CCSD(T), with all individual errors below 1 kcal/mol. Reaction profiles show that accurate endpoint energies can conceal substantial barrier errors, emphasizing the importance of approximating omitted correlation.

TITLE: The full Gaia colour-magnitude diagram with white dwarfs reveals a declining Milky Way star formation history
ABSTRACT: White dwarfs (WDs) provide a fossil record of the Milky Way star formation history (SFH) through the combined effects of stellar evolution and cooling. They have, however, generally been modelled in isolation from the non-degenerate population, so that no single self-consistent set of Galactic assumptions has been required to reproduce both. The calibrated model reproduces the star counts in each colour-magnitude diagram region to within 3% and the total number count to within 0.5%, compared with the 81% excess in the predicted WD count obtained with the previous main-sequence-only calibration. The recovered SFH peaks early, declines exponentially thereafter, and includes two recent bursts, located at $τ\approx2.4$ and $0.5$ Gyr. Three cooling libraries (Montréal, BaSTI and LPCODE) yield comparably good fits, with differences confined to ${\rm M_G}\gtrsim15$ mag. The predicted luminosity function is in reasonable agreement with the 100 pc Gaia WD sample. We further predict the Galactic WD distribution and differential number counts for surveys of varying depth. Extending the framework to the substellar regime using ATMO models, we predict the Galactic L, T, and Y dwarf populations and their contribution to the deep JWST fields.

TITLE: Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding
ABSTRACT: How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.

TITLE: Internalizing Investment Mandates: A Neurosymbolic RL Approach to Portfolio Allocation
ABSTRACT: Institutional portfolios operate under investment policy statements that specify asset allocation constraints, often conditioned on market regime information. Deep reinforcement learning has been shown to optimize financial performance but typically does not enforce rule compliance, making it unsuitable for real-world deployment. Existing neurosymbolic methods such as Probabilistic Logic Policy Gradient (PLPG) offer constraint-satisfying reinforcement learning (RL) via differentiable symbolic supervision but assume a finite, discrete action space. TITLE: Learning When Diffusion Language Models Should Regenerate
ABSTRACT: Remasking allows diffusion language models to revisit generated text, but effective correction requires knowing when and what to revise. Our diagnosis on Sudoku reveals two gaps. Hidden states contain error information that token confidence does not reliably expose, and remasking an isolated error usually reproduces it when the surrounding context remains fixed. TITLE: CoReMem: Cost-Internalizing Memory for Successive Coding Tasks with Prospective Valuation
ABSTRACT: Coding agents tackle successive tasks on an evolving repository, carrying forward edits and writing memory before the next task is known. Selecting memory for unknown next tasks requires balancing future usefulness against the costs of writing and reading it. The actor must also decide when acting on available memory saves effort as the task unfolds. We introduce Coding-Repository Memory (CoReMem), a cost-internalizing framework that guides memory design and actor adaptation through measured memory value on the next task. Prospective segment valuation compares runs of the next task with and without individual trajectory segments from the same inherited repository state, guiding a compact memory built around anchors: repository locations and executable checks. Actor adaptation estimates on-policy memory value within rollout groups with and without memory, using a reward that prioritizes resolution and penalizes step count in successful runs. Provenance-gated value credit assigns the additional value term to steps identified as using memory-supplied anchors, while every step retains outcome credit. On ChainSWE, CoReMem raises the 27B actor's resolve rate from 21.0% to 25.7%, with 17% fewer steps and 11% fewer total cache-aware tokens, including memory-module calls, relative to the untrained actor model without memory.

TITLE: Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks: Evidence from Additive Analogies in Meta-Learned Parameter Spaces
ABSTRACT: Humans can learn rapidly from limited experience, while neural networks typically require extensive training. In this work, we investigate what properties of a neural network can enable it to learn rapidly. We study neural networks that have been optimized via meta-learning, a technique that exposes a network to many related functions to facilitate its subsequent learning of similar functions. Prior work has shown that these meta-learned networks can learn new functions rapidly, but how they do so remains unclear. To investigate their internal structure, we take inspiration from classic work demonstrating additive relationships among the learned vectors that neural networks use to represent words (e.g., "king" minus "man" plus "woman" approximates "queen"). Analogously, we look for additive relationships among a meta-learned network's "representations" of different functions; when a meta-learned network rapidly learns to implement a specific function, all of its adapted parameter values can be concatenated into a single vector that we treat as representing the function. We find that such parameter vectors can be added and subtracted according to symbolic relationships among the functions they represent, yielding new parameter vectors that successfully represent the functions predicted by the corresponding symbolic manipulations: the networks specified by these artificially recombined vectors actually implement the intended functions without any direct training on them. Our results suggest that meta-learning can tune a network's starting point to organize subsequent learning along systematic, functionally meaningful trajectories through the space of possible parameter vectors, providing a geometric basis for realizing inductive biases in neural networks.

TITLE: Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution
ABSTRACT: Context attribution asks how removing parts of a prompt changes a language model's output. We study leave-one-out (LOO) attribution for a prompt of $n$ tokens and a fixed measurement function. Computing all LOO values directly requires $n$ model evaluations. Under a stability assumption, our algorithm estimates all LOO values to error proportional to $\varepsilon$ with constant probability using $O(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ evaluation calls. To complement our upper bound, we prove a matching $Ω(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ lower bound on the number of evaluation calls in an oracle model. Empirically, we verify coefficient decay consistent with stability and evaluate the performance of our sketching algorithm on a small language-model retrieval task. Technically, our result is based on a sketching algorithm for arithmetic circuits whose Taylor coefficients satisfy the stability condition recently introduced by Gunn [Gun26]. A direct adaptation of their forward differentiation sketches would require $\widetilde{O}(\log(n)/\varepsilon^2)$ evaluation calls. Our key observation is that we can apply forward differentiation to the backpropagation circuit, with only constant overhead over a forward sketch of the same Taylor degree. This reduces the variance of the sketches and is key to our improved evaluation bound. Our lower bound builds on the techniques of Amsel, Chen, Duman Keles, Halikias, Musco, and Musco [ACDH+26, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications] for lower bounding the number of matrix-vector queries to estimate the diagonal elements of random matrices.

ABSTRACT: Decoder-only Large View Synthesis Models (LVSMs) with KV-cache have recently achieved state-of-the-art quality by regressing novel views from neural networks without reconstructing 3D geometry, while Test-Time Training (TTT) layers save computation but sacrifice quality. We propose Test-Time Baking (TTB), a strategy that learns to bake global scene information from cross-view attention into a lightweight MLP, enabling fast and high-fidelity novel-view decoding. The first key is to repurpose the fast-weight MLP of TTT to learn the cross-view attention mapping explicitly from its input-output pairs, rather than replacing attention entirely, achieving the same rendering efficiency as TTT while delivering substantially higher quality. ABSTRACT: The JWST Rocky Worlds Director's Discretionary Time (RWDDT) program uses secondary eclipse observations to determine the conditions under which rocky exoplanets around M dwarfs retain their atmospheres. We present RWDDT MIRI/F1500W observations of LTT 1445 A b and c, the transiting rocky siblings in the nearest such system (6.9 pc), comprising two eclipses of each planet across three visits. TITLE: Consistent Video-to-Video Translation via Explicit Correspondences
ABSTRACT: Interactive video-to-video applications require real-time generation while maintaining long-range temporal consistency. TITLE: Scalable Pauli propagation through $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear partitioning
ABSTRACT: Pauli propagation simulates quantum circuits and many-body dynamics by evolving observables in the Heisenberg picture as sparse sums of Pauli strings. As Pauli-string operations reduce to bit manipulations, its performance is limited by memory bandwidth and capacity rather than computational throughput. Pooling the memory of many cores and nodes is hindered by the deduplication step after every gate, which can require global synchronization. We avoid this bottleneck by partitioning the strings with an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear hash function, which guarantees that a $k$-qubit channel couples each partition to at most $4^k$ partitions fixed by the gate, decomposing gate application into independent tasks with bounded communication. Benchmarks of our open-source implementation demonstrate efficient parallel scaling within and across compute nodes, enabling simulations with over $10^{11}$ Pauli strings and extending the accessible timescales of quantum many-body dynamics.

TITLE: Objects as Addresses: Instance Binding in Vision Transformer Attention
ABSTRACT: Humans naturally parse visual scenes into coherent individual objects rather than scattered features, a capability cognitive science calls object binding. While recent work shows that pretrained Vision Transformers (ViTs) intrinsically acquire this ability, how native self-attention computations mechanistically keep object instances separate remains unresolved. By studying this across eight pretrained ViTs, we found that instance separation is expressed more strongly in attention routing than in token content: selected query-key relations distinguish individual objects more reliably than value similarity or residual representations. Inside heads, patches belonging to one individual tend to read from a common image region, which we term their attention address. Visually distinct parts of one person share an address, whereas identical duplicated instances receive separate, movable addresses. These shared destinations arise in part because query interactions with the spatial structure of keys direct attention toward consistent regions for one object and elsewhere for another. In DINOv2 and most other backbones we tested, editing this query component at the selected head redirects its attention address toward another individual. TITLE: On the maximum of Poissonian log-correlated fields
ABSTRACT: We consider stationary log-correlated random fields of the form \[ X_n(t):= \sum_{j\le n} f(e^{τ_j} t+ v_{j})\,,\quad t\in \mathbb{R} \] for a centered 1-periodic analytic function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, where $(τ_j)_{j\ge1}$ are the arrival times of a rate-1 Poisson process on $\mathbb{R}_+$ and $(v_j)_{j\ge1}$ are iid uniform random variables in $[0,1)$. We show that with high probability, \[ \max_{t\in[-1,1]}X_n(t) = c_1 n + c_2 τ_n- (c_3+o(1))\log n \] where $c_1,c_2,c_3$ are positive constants depending on $f$. In particular, the random frequency $τ_n$ produces a central limit fluctuation of order $\sqrt{n}$, together with a logarithmic correction different from the ubiquitous Bramson correction for models with strong comparisons to Gaussian fields, such as homogeneous branching random walks and log-characteristic polynomials of C$β$E matrices. Our proof makes a connection with branching random walks in random time-environment, where coarse-scale fluctuations of the arrival times $τ_j$ play the role of a quenched random environment, and fine-scale fluctuations are leveraged to establish a branching decorrelation structure. We conjecture analogous asymptotics for the characteristic polynomials of random permutation matrices, and expect the same mechanism to arise in other Poissonian fields such as characteristic polynomials of sparse random matrices.

TITLE: Back to Pixels: Lifting Latent Video Diffusion into Pixel Space
ABSTRACT: Frontier video generation models work in a VAE latent space that compresses video in both space and time. This compression made training tractable, but now limits high-resolution, controllable, and interactive generation -- doubling resolution quadruples token count, conditions apply to groups of frames rather than individual ones, and each response must wait for the rest of its multi-frame block. We present PixVid, a simple yet effective method that lifts a pretrained latent video model to directly generate every frame in pixel space. PixVid keeps the diffusion transformer and replaces the VAE decoder with a convolutional head that renders its outputs into pixels. TITLE: Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution
ABSTRACT: Context attribution asks how removing parts of a prompt changes a language model's output. TITLE: Symmetry in Time: A Representation Theory of Linear Recurrent Memory
ABSTRACT: State space models (SSMs) are an emerging alternative to Transformers. SSMs offer linear-time inference and parallel training, but most implementations make the parallel scan efficient by making the recurrence diagonal: each channel of the hidden state is multiplied by an input-dependent real or complex number. These numbers commute, so the state records which inputs occurred but not their order. We formalize this with representation theory, which explains and fixes the state-tracking failures previously reported for SSMs. The state update defines a linear representation of the monoid of input sequences, and we show that an SSM tracks a group at every sequence length exactly when that group is a quotient of the group generated by its transition matrices. Two consequences follow. Diagonal real- and complex-valued SSMs can track only commutative groups, while the transition matrices of any SSM that can track a group must split into independent blocks over the real, complex, and quaternion numbers. We then introduce HyperSSM, which adds quaternion channels while keeping linear-time inference and parallel training. Trained only on final answers, HyperSSM solves the group tasks of recent state-tracking benchmarks exactly with at most five state numbers. This includes $S_5$ with 32 generators, where previous SSMs are at chance, and HyperSSM generalizes to sequences 25 times longer than those seen in training. Because the theory says what the transitions must encode, the learned weights of the linear recurrence can be interpreted directly. We demonstrate this by recovering the calibration of a real gyroscope, the rotations of imperfect qubit gates and the geometry of protein backbones.

TITLE: The full Gaia colour-magnitude diagram with white dwarfs reveals a declining Milky Way star formation history
ABSTRACT: White dwarfs (WDs) provide a fossil record of the Milky Way star formation history (SFH) through the combined effects of stellar evolution and cooling. TITLE: Ensemble phase transitions in measurement-unitary hybrid quantum circuits
ABSTRACT: The universal properties of state ensembles generated by quantum dynamics provide a refined lens into Hilbert-space ergodicity and deep thermalisation, or lack thereof. We investigate deep-ergodicity breaking transitions of an ancillary qubit entangled with a system undergoing dynamics via hybrid quantum circuits, where unitary evolution is interspersed with projective measurements. Specifically, we analyse the statistical properties of two distinct state ensembles: the trajectory ensemble (TE) conditioned on the measurement outcomes of the hybrid circuit, and the projected ensemble (PE) generated by further projective measurements of the system at the final temporal boundary. We find that both ensembles undergo non-analytic changes -- ensemble phase transitions -- at the critical point of the measurement-induced purification transition, despite their first moments remaining completely maximally mixed and blind to the transition. For Haar-random circuits, the TE transitions from a trivial identity ensemble to the uniform Haar ensemble, while the PE transitions from the Haar ensemble to a trivial singleton pure state. In Clifford circuits, absence of non-stabiliserness limits the PE to a $1$-design, while the TE forms a $k$-design for $k \leq 3$. We demonstrate that these genuine ensemble transitions offer a clear physical mechanism for the measurement-induced entanglement or purification transitions, illustrating how the capacity to ergodically steer a quantum subsystem shifts from the temporal boundary measurements in the mixed phase to the bulk trajectory measurements in the pure phase.

TITLE: The Cauchy problem for skew-torsion-parallel spinors with applications to supergravity
ABSTRACT: We solve the Cauchy problem for an irreducible real spinor parallel with respect to a metric connection with skew-symmetric torsion on a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold, proving that it is well-posed modulo an obstruction that we identify explicitly, and obtaining an initial-data characterization of such spinors. Our proof relies on the theory of spinorial forms, which we use to explicitly integrate the coupled flow for the spinor and underlying metric via a rotated pull-back action of the flow generated by a vector field on the Cauchy hypersurface $Σ$. We characterize the corresponding constraint equations as an explicit exterior differential system and describe all its solutions in terms of contact structures and flat foliations, obtaining a complete classification in the compact case. In particular, we conclude that every oriented compact three-manifold arises as the Cauchy hypersurface of a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor. Building on these results, we study the interaction between the four-dimensional Neveu-Schwarz supergravity evolution flow on an abelian bundle gerbe and the spinorial evolution flow determined by its supersymmetric conditions, which we prove to be well-posed and compatible. This establishes the well-posedness of the supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz system in four dimensions and provides an initial-data characterization of supersymmetry. Furthermore, we obtain a strong rigidity result stating that every globally hyperbolic supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz solution with a compact Cauchy hypersurface is flat and has vanishing bundle-gerbe curvature.

TITLE: Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution
ABSTRACT: Context attribution asks how removing parts of a prompt changes a language model's output. We study leave-one-out (LOO) attribution for a prompt of $n$ tokens and a fixed measurement function. Computing all LOO values directly requires $n$ model evaluations. Under a stability assumption, our algorithm estimates all LOO values to error proportional to $\varepsilon$ with constant probability using $O(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ evaluation calls. To complement our upper bound, we prove a matching $Ω(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ lower bound on the number of evaluation calls in an oracle model. Empirically, we verify coefficient decay consistent with stability and evaluate the performance of our sketching algorithm on a small language-model retrieval task. Technically, our result is based on a sketching algorithm for arithmetic circuits whose Taylor coefficients satisfy the stability condition recently introduced by Gunn [Gun26]. A direct adaptation of their forward differentiation sketches would require $\widetilde{O}(\log(n)/\varepsilon^2)$ evaluation calls. Our key observation is that we can apply forward differentiation to the backpropagation circuit, with only constant overhead over a forward sketch of the same Taylor degree. This reduces the variance of the sketches and is key to our improved evaluation bound. Our lower bound builds on the techniques of Amsel, Chen, Duman Keles, Halikias, Musco, and Musco [ACDH+26, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications] for lower bounding the number of matrix-vector queries to estimate the diagonal elements of random matrices.

ABSTRACT: The symmetries of a $d$-dimensional QFT are known to be captured by a topological theory in one dimension higher, the Symmetry Topological Field Theory (SymTFT), which is invariant under gauging finite symmetries of the QFT, but not under dynamical gauging of continuous symmetries. TITLE: Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks: Evidence from Additive Analogies in Meta-Learned Parameter Spaces
ABSTRACT: Humans can learn rapidly from limited experience, while neural networks typically require extensive training. In this work, we investigate what properties of a neural network can enable it to learn rapidly. We study neural networks that have been optimized via meta-learning, a technique that exposes a network to many related functions to facilitate its subsequent learning of similar functions. Prior work has shown that these meta-learned networks can learn new functions rapidly, but how they do so remains unclear. To investigate their internal structure, we take inspiration from classic work demonstrating additive relationships among the learned vectors that neural networks use to represent words (e.g., "king" minus "man" plus "woman" approximates "queen"). The calibrated model reproduces the star counts in each colour-magnitude diagram region to within 3% and the total number count to within 0.5%, compared with the 81% excess in the predicted WD count obtained with the previous main-sequence-only calibration. The recovered SFH peaks early, declines exponentially thereafter, and includes two recent bursts, located at $τ\approx2.4$ and $0.5$ Gyr. Three cooling libraries (Montréal, BaSTI and LPCODE) yield comparably good fits, with differences confined to ${\rm M_G}\gtrsim15$ mag. The predicted luminosity function is in reasonable agreement with the 100 pc Gaia WD sample. We further predict the Galactic WD distribution and differential number counts for surveys of varying depth. Extending the framework to the substellar regime using ATMO models, we predict the Galactic L, T, and Y dwarf populations and their contribution to the deep JWST fields.

TITLE: Back to Pixels: Lifting Latent Video Diffusion into Pixel Space
ABSTRACT: Frontier video generation models work in a VAE latent space that compresses video in both space and time. This compression made training tractable, but now limits high-resolution, controllable, and interactive generation -- doubling resolution quadruples token count, conditions apply to groups of frames rather than individual ones, and each response must wait for the rest of its multi-frame block. We present PixVid, a simple yet effective method that lifts a pretrained latent video model to directly generate every frame in pixel space. PixVid keeps the diffusion transformer and replaces the VAE decoder with a convolutional head that renders its outputs into pixels. We find that predicting clean video rather than velocity, together with a larger noise shift, is key to quality. Finetuned from Wan2.1-14B, PixVid is competitive with the latent baseline within $\sim 1.3$M training clips. Working framewise in pixels, it generates at $2\times$ higher resolution with the same per-frame token count, keeps depth-conditioned video editing aligned under fast motion, and responds to each action at the next frame. Project page: https://chenshuo20.github.io/PixVid/

TITLE: Ensemble phase transitions in measurement-unitary hybrid quantum circuits
ABSTRACT: The universal properties of state ensembles generated by quantum dynamics provide a refined lens into Hilbert-space ergodicity and deep thermalisation, or lack thereof. We investigate deep-ergodicity breaking transitions of an ancillary qubit entangled with a system undergoing dynamics via hybrid quantum circuits, where unitary evolution is interspersed with projective measurements. Specifically, we analyse the statistical properties of two distinct state ensembles: the trajectory ensemble (TE) conditioned on the measurement outcomes of the hybrid circuit, and the projected ensemble (PE) generated by further projective measurements of the system at the final temporal boundary. We find that both ensembles undergo non-analytic changes -- ensemble phase transitions -- at the critical point of the measurement-induced purification transition, despite their first moments remaining completely maximally mixed and blind to the transition. For Haar-random circuits, the TE transitions from a trivial identity ensemble to the uniform Haar ensemble, while the PE transitions from the Haar ensemble to a trivial singleton pure state. In Clifford circuits, absence of non-stabiliserness limits the PE to a $1$-design, while the TE forms a $k$-design for $k \leq 3$. We demonstrate that these genuine ensemble transitions offer a clear physical mechanism for the measurement-induced entanglement or purification transitions, illustrating how the capacity to ergodically steer a quantum subsystem shifts from the temporal boundary measurements in the mixed phase to the bulk trajectory measurements in the pure phase.

TITLE: Learning When Diffusion Language Models Should Regenerate
ABSTRACT: Remasking allows diffusion language models to revisit generated text, but effective correction requires knowing when and what to revise. Our diagnosis on Sudoku reveals two gaps. TITLE: The Cauchy problem for skew-torsion-parallel spinors with applications to supergravity
ABSTRACT: We solve the Cauchy problem for an irreducible real spinor parallel with respect to a metric connection with skew-symmetric torsion on a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold, proving that it is well-posed modulo an obstruction that we identify explicitly, and obtaining an initial-data characterization of such spinors. Our proof relies on the theory of spinorial forms, which we use to explicitly integrate the coupled flow for the spinor and underlying metric via a rotated pull-back action of the flow generated by a vector field on the Cauchy hypersurface $Σ$. We characterize the corresponding constraint equations as an explicit exterior differential system and describe all its solutions in terms of contact structures and flat foliations, obtaining a complete classification in the compact case. In particular, we conclude that every oriented compact three-manifold arises as the Cauchy hypersurface of a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor. Building on these results, we study the interaction between the four-dimensional Neveu-Schwarz supergravity evolution flow on an abelian bundle gerbe and the spinorial evolution flow determined by its supersymmetric conditions, which we prove to be well-posed and compatible. This establishes the well-posedness of the supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz system in four dimensions and provides an initial-data characterization of supersymmetry. Furthermore, we obtain a strong rigidity result stating that every globally hyperbolic supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz solution with a compact Cauchy hypersurface is flat and has vanishing bundle-gerbe curvature.

TITLE: Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks: Evidence from Additive Analogies in Meta-Learned Parameter Spaces
ABSTRACT: Humans can learn rapidly from limited experience, while neural networks typically require extensive training. In this work, we investigate what properties of a neural network can enable it to learn rapidly. We study neural networks that have been optimized via meta-learning, a technique that exposes a network to many related functions to facilitate its subsequent learning of similar functions. Prior work has shown that these meta-learned networks can learn new functions rapidly, but how they do so remains unclear. To investigate their internal structure, we take inspiration from classic work demonstrating additive relationships among the learned vectors that neural networks use to represent words (e.g., "king" minus "man" plus "woman" approximates "queen"). Analogously, we look for additive relationships among a meta-learned network's "representations" of different functions; when a meta-learned network rapidly learns to implement a specific function, all of its adapted parameter values can be concatenated into a single vector that we treat as representing the function. We find that such parameter vectors can be added and subtracted according to symbolic relationships among the functions they represent, yielding new parameter vectors that successfully represent the functions predicted by the corresponding symbolic manipulations: the networks specified by these artificially recombined vectors actually implement the intended functions without any direct training on them. Our results suggest that meta-learning can tune a network's starting point to organize subsequent learning along systematic, functionally meaningful trajectories through the space of possible parameter vectors, providing a geometric basis for realizing inductive biases in neural networks.
